Why tsavorite deserves to be seen in person

Tsavorite is a green variety of grossular garnet. Its colour can move from a fresh, lively green to a deeper forest green, sometimes with a slight yellow or blue influence. The finest stone is not automatically the darkest. If a tsavorite becomes too dark, it can lose life.

What I look for is a balance of hue, tone, saturation, clarity and brilliance—a green that remains alive as the gemstone moves. Photographs can introduce you to a piece, but cameras, screens and editing can all change the way green is represented. A photograph should therefore be the beginning of your decision, not the end.

A knowledgeable jeweller should be willing to show you the gemstone under more than one light source, explain what you are seeing and allow you to compare options without pressure. View it under jewellery lighting, ordinary indoor light and, where possible, indirect daylight.

My rule: a fine tsavorite should not merely be green. It should feel alive.

What exactly is tsavorite?

Tsavorite is the green variety of grossular garnet. Its green colour is associated with trace elements within the crystal, while the unusual geological conditions required for its formation help explain its scarcity. The Gemological Institute of America’s garnet description identifies tsavorite as grossular garnet and notes the exceptional conditions required for its formation.

The gemstone is valued for its vivid colour, brilliance and combination of beauty and wearability. It measures approximately 7 to 7.5 on the Mohs hardness scale. That makes it suitable for rings, earrings, pendants and bracelets when it is properly cut, securely set and worn with sensible care.

Fine tsavorite is commonly found in smaller sizes, and exceptional stones become increasingly difficult to find as size, colour, clarity and cutting quality improve together. GIA’s garnet quality factors provide useful independent guidance on colour, clarity, cut and carat weight.

Tsavorite is often untreated, but a buyer should avoid absolute assumptions. Rare fracture filling has been encountered in garnet. For a significant gemstone, rely on the findings for that specific stone rather than a general statement about the gemstone variety.

How I judge a fine tsavorite

There is no single number that captures beauty. I assess the following qualities together.

1. Colour: look for life, not darkness

Colour is the first impression. I look for a green that is vivid, balanced and attractive in different environments. A stone may look spectacular under jewellery lighting but flatter in daylight. The face-up appearance matters more than a poetic colour description—the gemstone must prove itself to your eye.

Ask yourself:

  • Does the green look lively or heavy?
  • Is the colour evenly distributed when viewed face-up?
  • Does the gemstone retain its appeal away from intense display lighting?
  • Do I still love the colour when I move the stone?

2. Cut: brilliance must not be sacrificed merely to retain weight

Tsavorite rough can be irregular, and fine material is valuable. This can tempt a cutter to retain as much weight as possible, but carat weight alone does not create beauty.

Look straight through the centre of the gemstone. A pale or transparent area can indicate a window, where light passes through rather than returning to your eye. Large dark areas that remain black as the stone moves may indicate excessive extinction. A skilful cut should release the colour and energy of the gem while preserving sensible weight.

3. Clarity: understand what is natural and what is distracting

Natural inclusions are part of a gemstone’s formation. The question is not whether a tsavorite is completely empty under magnification; it is whether inclusions distract from its beauty or affect durability.

For a ring, pay particular attention to fractures near corners, points or the girdle. For earrings and pendants, the stone faces less daily impact, but vulnerable areas still need to be protected by the setting.

4. Carat weight: rarity rises, but beauty remains the priority

Fine tsavorite becomes more difficult to find as size increases. A larger gemstone is not automatically better. A smaller tsavorite with superior colour and cut can be far more beautiful than a larger stone that is dark, windowed or poorly proportioned.

Buy the finest combination your budget allows. Beauty before a number on a scale.

5. The setting: it must protect and reveal the gemstone

Gemstone knowledge and jewellery design cannot be separated. The setting should secure the tsavorite, protect vulnerable areas and allow its green colour to remain expressive.

Metal colour changes the visual experience. Yellow gold can add warmth, while white gold or platinum creates a cooler contrast. At Shimansky, our approach to design and craft considers the gemstone, proportion, comfort, durability and finishing as one complete design.

Certification, ethical assurance and independent reports: understand the difference

The word certified is often used too loosely in jewellery. At Shimansky, there are three distinct layers a buyer should understand.

Independent coloured-stone laboratory reportA recognised independent gemological laboratoryWhere applicable for a significant gemstone, this can independently identify the gemstone and record characteristics or detectable treatments within the laboratory’s scope

Every Shimansky tsavorite comes with the first two forms of assurance. They are Shimansky-issued commitments and should not be represented as independent laboratory reports.

Independent reporting is a separate level of verification. A respected gemological laboratory can identify the gemstone, state whether it is natural or laboratory-grown, and record any detectable treatments. Its report may also include shape, measurements, weight, colour and a photograph. GIA’s sample coloured-stone identification report is a useful example of the scope of independent reporting.

An independent report does not decide which tsavorite is more beautiful, guarantee financial value, certify the jeweller or replace a jewellery warranty. It verifies gemological findings within the scope of the report.

For a significant centre stone, ask whether an independent report is already available or should be arranged. Small pavé-set tsavorites are not normally submitted one by one because individual reports would be impractical. Documentation therefore depends on the design, stone size and nature of the purchase.

Before buying, ask:

  • Can I see the Shimansky Certificate of Authenticity for this piece?
  • Does this piece include its Ethical Compliance Assurance?
  • Is there a separate independent laboratory report for the centre stone?
  • Which laboratory issued it?
  • Does the report correspond to the exact gemstone I am buying?
  • Can its report number be verified with the laboratory?
  • Does it disclose any detectable treatment?

A trustworthy answer should be specific, clear and supported by the relevant document.

What a reliable jewellery warranty should mean

A warranty is valuable only when its terms are understandable and the jeweller remains available to honour it.

Every Shimansky jewellery creation is accompanied by a lifetime jewellery warranty. If an element becomes loose or breaks through normal wear, Shimansky craftsmen will assess the piece and restore or replace the qualifying element in accordance with the applicable written warranty terms.

Accidental impact, loss, misuse and routine maintenance are not necessarily the same as a manufacturing or qualifying normal-wear issue. Ask for the written terms applying to your piece and retain the purchase documentation.

Professional inspections remain important. Prongs and fine settings wear gradually, particularly on rings. A timely inspection can identify movement before a gemstone is lost. Contact Shimansky to arrange cleaning, maintenance or an assessment.

How to care for tsavorite jewellery

Tsavorite is durable enough for jewellery, but it is not indestructible.

  • Remove rings before gym training, gardening, heavy lifting or household work.
  • Avoid hard knocks, especially around corners, points and the girdle.
  • Clean the piece with warm water, mild soap and a soft brush.
  • Rinse carefully and dry with a soft, lint-free cloth.
  • Avoid steam cleaning.
  • Do not use ultrasonic cleaning if the gemstone has fractures, has been fracture-filled or has not been professionally inspected.
  • Store the piece separately so harder gemstones, particularly diamonds, cannot scratch its metal or neighbouring gemstones.
  • Have rings and frequently worn pieces inspected periodically.

These recommendations are consistent with GIA’s garnet care and cleaning guide.
